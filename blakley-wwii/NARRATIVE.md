# Pvt. Melbourne R. Blakley, 34864047: Company L, 16th Infantry, 1st Infantry Division

*Compiled 11 November 2026 from U.S. Army morning reports and attached special orders in the
National Archives Catalog (Series NAID 85713825, "Morning Reports").*

Every sentence below is labeled:

- **[DOC]**: stated in a document I located and read. The roll and image number are in
  [`research_log.csv`](research_log.csv), and copies of the pages are in [`evidence/`](evidence/).
- **[CO]**: stated in Company L's own morning reports about the *company*. He was a member of
  the company on these dates, but he is not named individually.
- **[CONTEXT]**: general history of the 16th Infantry or the Army replacement system. This is
  **not** a record of him personally.
- **[INFERENCE]**: my reasoning from the documents. Treat it as a lead, not a fact.

**The records do not support the family story that he arrived in Normandy as a replacement
shortly after D-Day.** He reported to the Fort Bragg Reception Center on 23 May 1944, two
weeks before D-Day. He then trained at Camp Croft, S.C., until early October 1944 [DOC]. He
joined Company L in Germany in late November 1944, during the fighting near the Hürtgen
Forest east of Aachen. The confusion is understandable. The 16th Infantry is famous for
Omaha Beach, and for decades all veterans of the regiment were associated with D-Day.

## 1. Into the Army: Fort Bragg, May 1944

**23 May 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 123 of the Headquarters Reception Center, Fort Bragg,
North Carolina, lists "34864047 Blakley, Melbourne R. Pvt." among men "having reported to this
Reception Center this date." Most of the men on that page have serial numbers within a few
digits of his (348640xx).

[CONTEXT] Serial numbers beginning with 3 belonged to men inducted through Selective Service
(draftees). The 34- block was issued in the Fourth Service Command, which included North
Carolina. [INFERENCE] Men with consecutive numbers were normally inducted together at the same
induction station. His group, which includes names such as Fulp, Tise, Sprinkle and Pinnix,
fits a Winston-Salem/Forsyth County induction. The enlistment database (Step 1) would confirm
his induction date and place.

## 2. Infantry training: Camp Croft, South Carolina, May to October 1944

**31 May 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 130, Infantry Replacement Training Center (IRTC),
Camp Croft, S.C.: men "having reported to this sta fr RC, Ft Bragg, N.C., are attached
unassigned in gr of Pvt to orgn indicated, effective 31 May 44." He is listed under **Co C,
36th Infantry Training Battalion (ITB)**.

**25 August 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 204, IRTC Camp Croft: he and his group of
trainees were transferred to **Co C, 33rd ITB, "for completion of tng,"** effective 27 Aug 44
"(Sunday, before breakfast)."

**29 September 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 234, par. 41, IRTC Camp Croft: he was relieved
and transferred to **AGF Replacement Depot No. 1, Fort George G. Meade, Maryland**, as a
trained rifleman ("Infantry Rifle Co Tnd - MOS 745"). His line (no. 237) gives his Army General
Classification Test grade as **II**, his training company as **C 33**, a **delay en route of
11 days**, and a reporting date of **18 Oct 44**. [INFERENCE] The 11-day delay en route was
almost certainly a last home leave before going overseas.

**6 October 1944.** [DOC] The morning report of Co C, 33rd ITB records "28 EM ... dy to DS
enroute to AGF Pers Repl Depot #1 Ft Geo G Meade Md ... Par 41 SO 234". This is his group
leaving Camp Croft.

[CONTEXT] The IRTC course at this point in the war lasted about 17 weeks. He trained for
about 18 weeks (31 May to early October).

## 3. Overseas replacement pipeline: October to November 1944

**13 October 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 292, AGF Replacement Depot No. 1, Fort Meade,
transfers him into **Co C, 29th Battalion** of the depot: "BLAKLEY MELBOURNE 34864047 PVT 745".

**October 1944 (page undated).** [DOC] A Fort Meade outgoing shipment roster lists him as
no. 42 of **"GK555 (a) A INF CO E 1ST PLAT – RIFLE TRAINED"**, a replacement company under
2nd Lt Henry B. Fraas. Many of the same men stay with him through every later document.

**November 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 235, **Eleventh Replacement Depot, APO 131**: men
"having rptd this sta [for] Shpmt GK-555 (a)" are assigned to Det 102, GFRS (492nd Replacement
Company), Infantry Co "E", 1st to 4th Platoons, including Blakley. The day of the month on
this page is unreadable in the OCR, and I did not resolve it on the image.

**19 November 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 240, Eleventh Replacement Depot: Infantry Co E
of shipment GK 555(a), with him in the 1st Platoon, is transferred to the **17th Replacement
Depot, APO 873** "for Further Reasgnt."

**20–21 November 1944.** [DOC, quoted in SO 105] He was assigned to the 16th Infantry on
21 Nov 1944 from the **92nd Replacement Battalion, 3rd Replacement Depot**, per that
battalion's SO 156 of 20 Nov 1944.

[CONTEXT] GFRS means Ground Force Reinforcement System, the Army's chain of replacement depots
in Britain and on the Continent. The 3rd Replacement Depot fed First Army units, which
included the 1st Infantry Division. [INFERENCE] The documents do not say when he sailed, which
port he used, or where APO 131 and APO 873 were. The two-day moves between depots (19 → 20 →
21 Nov) suggest he was already on the Continent by mid-November. Treat this as unconfirmed.

## 4. Company L, 16th Infantry: Germany, 21–30 November 1944

**23 November 1944.** [DOC] Special Orders No. 105, Headquarters 16th Infantry (signed by
Capt. Lincoln D. Fish, Adjutant, "By order of Colonel Gibb"), assigns the new men to
companies. Under the heading **COMPANY "L" 16TH INFANTRY** is: **"Pvt MELBOURNE R. BLAKLEY,
34864047 — 745."** On the copy attached to Company L's report, the other companies' lists are
crossed out.

[DOC] The **Company L morning report dated 23 November 1944**, at Heistern, Germany, reads:
*"47 EM Repl asgd to & jd Co fr 3rd Repl Deppot as of 21 Nov 44 See atchd Copy of Par 1 SO
105 Hq 16th Inf dtd 23 Nov 44."* He is one of those 47 replacements.

[CO] The company's record of events for that same day is grim. The company got up at 0200,
moved from Hamich to Heistern, and attacked high ground at 0730. Small-arms fire and artillery
stopped the attack, and the company commander was wounded. Lt. Klenk, the only officer left,
reorganized the survivors into "one pltn of two squads." A combined infantry–tank assault at
1530 took the objective. German counterattacks followed at 1800 and again with tanks at 2100.
They were stopped only when division artillery was brought down on the company's own
positions. *"Total casulaties for the day – 2 Off LWA, 7 EM KIA, 19 EM WIA."* Six more men were
later reported missing as of 23 November.

[INFERENCE] The documents do not say whether the 47 replacements were in that attack or
arrived in the evening to fill the ranks. The company had 124 enlisted men assigned at the end
of 23 November. Another 76 replacements were added on 26 November.

[CO] **24–26 Nov:** defensive positions near Langerwehe under heavy shelling. On the night of
26 Nov the company was relieved and trucked to a bivouac east of Vicht. **27–29 Nov:**
reorganization. On 28 Nov *"all replacements went to Hamich to fire their wpns,"* and on 29 Nov
they finished on the range. [INFERENCE] He would have been among these replacements.
**30 Nov:** the company marched about 7 miles to Hucheln and was billeted in apartment houses.
Strength was 195 enlisted men.

[CONTEXT] This was the First Army's November offensive (Operation Queen, from 16 Nov 1944).
The 1st Infantry Division attacked east from the Stolberg–Aachen area through the northern
edge of the Hürtgen Forest toward Langerwehe and the Roer River. The fighting around Hamich,
Heistern and Langerwehe was among the costliest the division had since Normandy. That is why
the company needed more than 120 replacements in one week.

## 5. December 1944: Lammersdorf, Dison, and the Battle of the Bulge

All of the following is the company's own record [CO]. **No entry in Company L's November
(after the 23rd) or December reports names him**, which normally means his status did not
change. He stayed on duty with the company. See "What the silence means" below.

- **1–4 Dec, Hucheln, Germany:** training with rifle grenades and rocket launchers, baths and
  clean clothes. Enemy aircraft were overhead and one was shot down.
- **5–9 Dec, Lammersdorf, Germany:** trucked about 15 miles and relieved C Co, 60th Infantry
  (9th Division) in the woods. Defensive positions under enemy observation; wiring and
  sandbagging positions.
- **10–16 Dec, Dison, Belgium:** rest area. Drill, a Red Cross Clubmobile with coffee and
  doughnuts, showers, movies, and passes for half the company.
- **17 Dec:** alerted in the morning. Trucked about 26 miles to **Robertville, Belgium**,
  arriving at 2258, and dug in that night. [CONTEXT] The German Ardennes offensive (the Battle
  of the Bulge) had begun on 16 December. The 1st Division was rushed south to hold the
  northern shoulder of the breakthrough.
- **18–24 Dec, Weywertz, Belgium:** road blocks south of Weywertz on the main highway. On about
  20 Dec a strong pre-dawn attack was held; at daylight the company *"captured 43 and killed
  about the same number."* On 21 Dec the 3rd Platoon ambushed an enemy company marching down
  the road. Tank, artillery and mortar fire followed. Six men were reported missing as of
  20 Dec.
- **25 Dec, Weywertz (in reserve):** *"Did not do anything except celebrate Christmas."* An
  officer's patrol that night lost Lt. McLaughlin.
- **26–29 Dec:** reserve, weapons classes and patrols.
- **30–31 Dec:** moved after dark into new defensive positions near "Gueuzaine" (the place name
  is uncertain in the OCR), with no movement in daylight because of enemy observation.

[CONTEXT] The 16th Infantry's positions around Weywertz and Bütgenbach were part of the
northern shoulder of the Bulge (the Elsenborn Ridge area). There the 1st, 2nd, 99th and 9th
Infantry Divisions stopped the German attempt to swing north toward Liège.

### What the silence means

A morning report lists a soldier by name only when something changes: he joins, leaves, is
wounded, gets sick, goes missing, is promoted, and so on. From 24 Nov to 31 Dec 1944 I found
**no entry naming Blakley or 34864047** in Company L's reports, using:

1. NARA's catalog OCR (search and page match for "Blakley", the serial, and loose variants), and
2. a second, independent OCR pass over the downloaded images (`tools/reocr.py`) with fuzzy
   matching on the name and serial.

[INFERENCE] The most likely reading is that he remained on duty with Company L through the end
of 1944. **Confidence is medium, not high.** Several December frames, especially 17–22 Dec,
are faint, creased and water-damaged, and both OCR engines lose text on them. All 51 December
and 24 November–30 November page images are downloaded, so a human can read each one.

## 6. 1945 and after (not yet documented)

The National Archives Catalog has no digitized 1945 morning reports. I checked, and no 1945
rolls exist in the series online. [CONTEXT] In 1945 the 1st Infantry Division attacked out of
the Bulge in January, pushed through the Siegfried Line, crossed the Roer in late February,
fought in the Remagen bridgehead and the Ruhr Pocket in March–April, cleared the Harz
Mountains in April, and ended the war in Czechoslovakia (around Cheb) in May 1945. **Whether he
was with the company for all of this is not yet known.**

## What to do next

1. **1945 morning reports (NPRC, St. Louis).** Ask for Company L, 16th Infantry, January–June
   1945 (or later), and give the serial number **34864047**. An independent researcher at NPRC
   can scan these. Searching by serial number speeds things up a lot.
2. **His discharge paper.** Many North Carolina veterans recorded their WD AGO 53-55
   (Honorable Discharge / Separation Qualification Record) with the **county Register of
   Deeds**. Check the **Forsyth County Register of Deeds** military discharge books. That form
   lists campaigns, decorations, wounds, the date he left for the ETO, and the date he returned.
3. **Enlistment record (Step 1).** The NARA AAD "WWII Army Enlistment Records" site refused
   automated access from this environment (HTTP 403). Search it in a normal browser at
   aad.archives.gov for serial **34864047**. It should give his induction date and place,
   birth year, education and civilian occupation.
4. **Hospital admission cards.** The AAD "WWII Hospital Admission Card Files" are searchable
   by serial number. They would show any wound or illness that sent him to a hospital,
   including in 1945.
5. **Awards.** Riflemen who saw combat were normally awarded the Combat Infantryman Badge by
   regimental general orders, and many later received the Bronze Star for the CIB. The 16th
   Infantry Regiment Association and the First Division Museum at Cantigny (which holds 1st
   Division records and photographs) can help.
6. **Faint December pages.** Read images 741–752 of December 1944 Roll 664 by eye, using the
   enhanced JPEGs the downloader creates.
